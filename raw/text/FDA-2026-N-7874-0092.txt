To the FDA Center for Devices and Radiological Health:<br/><br/>I am submitting comments on Considerations for the Regulation of Generative AI-Enabled Medical Devices. My comments focus primarily on Questions 19, 21, 22, and 26 concerning clinician review, postmarket monitoring, reassessment after modification, and agentic AI systems.<br/><br/>I am an independent researcher working on responsibility architecture for AI-native professional systems. My attached paper develops the Expert Oversight Network (EON), a framework for making professional human judgment a traceable and risk-calibrated part of AI deployment.<br/><br/>1. Clinician review should combine random sampling with risk-based escalation.<br/><br/>Periodic clinician review is a strong postmarket monitoring mechanism, but a single fixed sampling rate will not adequately reflect clinical risk.<br/><br/>Random sampling can establish baseline error rates and reveal unexpected failure patterns. Risk-weighted review should increase oversight when outputs become patient-specific, highly actionable, uncertain, contraindication-sensitive, or capable of materially influencing treatment or triage.<br/><br/>High-risk outputs may require pre-delivery review. Outputs that directly authorize or materially condition consequential clinical action should require explicit human sign-off.<br/><br/>Review status should also remain visible and machine-readable, for example: unreviewed, sampled, reviewed-confirmed, reviewed-corrected, conflict-open, escalated, or signed off.<br/><br/>2. External clinician participation should strengthen monitoring without diffusing manufacturer responsibility.<br/><br/>Clinicians, healthcare institutions, professional societies, and independent reviewers can provide valuable postmarket verification if responsibility remains tied to actual control.<br/><br/>The manufacturer should remain responsible for device architecture, risk-routing logic, review-state labeling, reviewer credential controls, change management, and audit infrastructure.<br/><br/>The deployer should remain responsible for the selected clinical use, local workflow integration, configured thresholds, and decisions to bypass required review gates.<br/><br/>A clinician reviewer should be responsible only for the defined scope actually reviewed or signed off.<br/><br/>This structure allows distributed professional review while preserving a clear responsibility anchor.<br/><br/>3. Postmarket monitoring should support incident reconstruction, not merely log accumulation.<br/><br/>When a consequential event occurs, regulators and manufacturers should be able to reconstruct the path from relevant evidence and patient context to AI output, professional review, correction or disagreement, final release state, human sign-off where required, and downstream action.<br/><br/>Record density can scale with risk. Routine low-risk interactions may require lightweight operational records. High-risk individualized advice should preserve provenance, reviewer scope, corrections, unresolved conflicts, and release authority. Critical outputs should support full evidence preservation.<br/><br/>4. Material changes in authority or execution should trigger proportionate reevaluation.<br/><br/>Reevaluation should be considered when changes materially alter how the device receives information, exercises authority, or affects clinical action. Relevant changes may include a new foundation model, persistent memory, external clinical data source, tool integration, agent capability, reviewer threshold, or downstream execution pathway.<br/><br/>This becomes particularly important for agentic systems. The ability to generate a recommendation, the authority assigned to that recommendation, and permission to execute a consequential action should remain distinct. High-consequence transitions should have explicit human authorization gates and traceable records.<br/><br/>I have attached Frame Infrastructure for AI, Vol. 3: Expert Oversight Network: Traceable Human Judgment for AI-Native Professional Systems, which develops these mechanisms in greater technical detail.<br/><br/>As GenAI-enabled medical devices become more capable and agentic, effective human oversight should specify what is reviewed, by whom, under what authority, at what risk threshold, and with what evidence preserved when failure occurs.<br/><br/>Thank you for your consideration.<br/><br/>Respectfully,<br/><br/>Xiangyu Guo<br/>Independent Researcher<br/>Alhambra, CA<br/><br/>Attachment:<br/>Frame Infrastructure for AI, Vol. 3: Expert Oversight Network: Traceable Human Judgment for AI-Native Professional Systems
