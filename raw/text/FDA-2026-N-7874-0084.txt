I am a junior at the University of Texas at Austin, premed, studying biology with a business minor. I work with HealthMaze, a nonprofit that runs on-site blood pressure screenings at food pantries and shelters in Austin and connects people to free or low-cost clinic referrals. I&#39;m writing to comment on how GenAI-enabled care escalation functions should weigh under-escalation against over-escalation.<br/><br/>In our screenings, this trade-off shows up with borderline readings. Under-referring risks missing something serious in a population that often has no other contact with the healthcare system. But over-referring for every borderline case has real costs for our patients too. It&#39;s a real cost for these people to take the time and spend the money traveling to clinics, and it can also make them distrust future screenings if it happens repeatedly, which discourages them from engaging with the healthcare system at all.<br/><br/>I think the right balance depends heavily on context, including how difficult it is to access the resource, how much benefit it would bring the population, and how much trust the population already has in the system. I&#39;d encourage CDRH to require that manufacturers justify their calibration of escalation thresholds against the specific population and care access context the device is deployed in, rather than applying a single acceptable error rate across settings with very different resource constraints.<br/><br/>Thank you for the opportunity to comment.
